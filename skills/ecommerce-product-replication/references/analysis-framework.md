# Competitor analysis framework

## 1. Executive judgment

- What the product is really selling beyond its physical form.
- Primary conversion mechanism.
- Strongest defensible advantage.
- Largest conversion weakness.
- Confidence and evidence limitations.

## 2. Product positioning

Segment audiences into primary buyer, secondary buyer, user, purchaser, and gift giver where relevant. Describe observable situations instead of stereotypes.

## 3. Page strategy

Analyze in actual page order:

- Main-image sequence and first-screen promise.
- Title keyword hierarchy.
- Selling-point order and information density.
- Detail-page narrative: recognition, mechanism, proof, product, usage, offer, objections, close.
- Promotion mechanics: anchor price, bundle, gift, threshold, urgency, risk reversal.
- Mobile readability and visual consistency.

## 4. User feedback

Separate observed review evidence from inferred concerns.

- Positive purchase reasons.
- Negative pain points.
- Pre-purchase concerns.
- High-frequency user phrases.
- Usage context and unexpected use cases.
- Packaging, logistics, taste/texture, service, value, and repeat-purchase signals.

Record sample size, review range, sorting method, and access limitations. Do not present a handful of highlighted reviews as representative of all buyers.

## 5. Competitive strength

Compare:

- Specification and per-unit price.
- Bundle and gift value.
- Formula/material and product experience.
- Trust evidence and its validity.
- Brand, channel, service, and supply advantages.
- Differentiation that is meaningful, provable, and visible on-page.

## 6. Advertising value

Map each sellable idea to a format:

| Angle | Consumer trigger | Main image | Short video | Seeding content | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Prioritize ideas that are understandable in three seconds, visually demonstrable, compliant, and connected to the offer.

## 7. Replication judgment

Classify findings:

- `Reuse`: general commercial structure or proven information order.
- `Adapt`: mechanism is useful but must fit the new product.
- `Test`: plausible but insufficient evidence.
- `Avoid`: protected, misleading, weak, or incompatible.

End with a causal model: traffic promise -> page proof -> offer -> objection handling -> conversion.
